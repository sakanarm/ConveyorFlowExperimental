# Response to the two major-revision concerns

Manuscript revision: bounded v2.6, 11 October 2026. This is a response draft for adviser review, not a claim that a journal has accepted the paper. The IEEE and AJSTR v2.6 Word files in `manuscripts_bounded_v2_6/` contain the corresponding Methods, Results, Discussion, limitations, and conclusion text. The earlier simulation and 60-case real-LLM cohorts are retained as separate studies; results are not silently pooled.

## Concern 1 — microtasks and repair surrogates do not establish executable ML or repository repair

**Response.** We agree. We added a separate executable-workload validation cohort. Eleven independently audited paired blocks each contained two jobs: a complete four-stage ML DAG and a repository repair checked by task-specific tests in a container. The same jobs were presented to CF-Fit, rule-matched central allocation, and static ownership under the same 3,600-second horizon. In this cohort, CF-Fit verified 9/11 ML pipelines and 8/11 repository repairs (17/22 total), the central arm 8/11 and 6/11 (14/22), and static ownership 9/11 and 9/11 (18/22). Observed verified throughput was 1.545, 1.273, and 1.636 jobs/hour, respectively. These are actual executable jobs, not exact-match microtasks. We revised the Abstract, Methods, Results, and limitations to identify which claims rely on the earlier surrogate cohort and which rely on this executable cohort.

**Scope and integrity.** The paired block—not the provider call, stage, or individual bug—is the comparison unit. The 159 provider attempts are not 159 independent observations. The cohort contains only four source repositories and two ML corpora, reused across blocks. One planned block stopped with two unresolved provider outcomes and was excluded without replay or imputation. Removing a partially exposed first block leaves 15/20, 12/20, and 16/20 verified jobs in the same arm order. These data show feasibility and bounded observed trade-offs; they do not establish performance over a population of repositories or ML datasets. The later two-block v2.5 recovery extension is exploratory and has not been pooled with the 11-block cohort.

**Evidence:** `major_revision_v2_4/MAIN_RESULTS_V2_4_AUDITED_TH.md`, `major_revision_v2_4/results/main_resume_v3/analysis.json`, retained block-audit capsules and raw local ledgers.

## Concern 2 — the CF-Fit versus Central-Fit comparison does not isolate decentralization

**Response.** We agree that the live-LLM contrast alone does not identify a pure decision-locus effect: assignment trajectories and provider latency can differ after the first decision. We therefore added a separate matched decision-layer experiment. Both architectures used the same three-task READY belt, Rank 1/2/3 team, fit and stand-down rules, SQLite atomic claim, verifier fixtures, and fixed service outcomes. The sole architectural change was whether the allocation decision ran in the agent process or in a central coordinator process. Twenty tie seeds crossed four fault conditions and two arms (160 audited, no-LLM trials). Both arms verified 3/3 tasks without faults. During a coordinator-only outage, CF-Fit verified 3/3 and the central arm 0/3; during a shared belt/claim-store outage, both verified 0/3; during an agent-A2 outage, both verified 2/3. All scenarios returned to 3/3 after recovery. The no-fault proposal/claim parity and fault evidence were audited.

**Scope and integrity.** The twenty seeds vary tie-breaking on the same task stream; they do not represent twenty independent workload samples. The result supports only the claim that CF-Fit removes dependence on a central *allocation-decision process* in the tested fault window. It does not imply an end-to-end absence of single points of failure: the shared belt, claim store, verifier, and provider gateway remain potential common dependencies. It does not estimate a production outage rate or prove that CF-Fit has superior real-LLM throughput under faults. We removed the earlier statement that no fault injection had been performed and explicitly distinguish this controlled experiment from the live LLM comparison.

**Evidence:** `major_revision_v2_4/DECISION_FAULT_RESULT_TH.md`, its frozen lock and independently audited result ledger; the earlier 2,160-run matched simulation is separately described in `major_revision_v2_3/MATCHED_ARCHITECTURE_RESULTS_TH.md` and is not presented as a live fault result.

## RQ-aligned interpretation

RQ1 is a vector of throughput, time, utilization, cost, and outcome quality—not a claim that CF-Fit wins every metric. In the executable cohort CF-Fit verified more jobs than the rule-matched central arm but fewer than static ownership. Conditional completion time compares different successful subsets. Busy utilization includes provider waiting. Monetary cost per verified job is unavailable because each arm has one or more unknown-cost attempts; unknown values are not replaced by zero. Provider-reported known cost units are not asserted to be a verified currency.

RQ2's homogeneous-versus-heterogeneous causal question is evaluated in the frozen simulation with mean-ability-matched teams. The live homogeneous extension is an ecological boundary check, not a mean-matched causal estimate. Ablations test fit, local assessment, aging, and stand-down; the thirteen experimental policies are not framed as thirteen scientific contributions. Throughout, tasks become READY when dependencies finish, ride the common belt, and are selected by volunteering agents. Atomic claim resolves collisions but does not choose the best agent.

## Residual limitations and editorial decision

The two original objections are addressed **for a bounded feasibility and decision-layer claim**. They are not fully closed for claims about cross-repository generalization, population equivalence/superiority, complete monetary cost efficiency, or system-wide fault tolerance. The prospective v2.6 source-diversity protocol remains separate and should be run before any stronger claim. Sanic container eligibility has only been established for two candidate cases; these are not yet main real-LLM replicates. PySnooper candidates failed the current buggy-fail/fixed-pass preflight. No v2.6 provider calls are counted here.

Suggested editorial wording: “The revision replaces surrogate-only claims with a separately audited executable cohort and isolates decision-layer dependence through a matched fault experiment. We have restricted our claims to the tested workloads and fault window, and disclose the unresolved external-validity and cost-accounting limitations.”
